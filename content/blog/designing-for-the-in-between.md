The best interfaces are often remembered for the moments between the big interactions. A search with no results. A button that makes it clear something was copied. The reassuring layout of a page that hasn't loaded yet.

## Start where nothing happens

Empty states deserve real copy. Instead of a blank column, explain what the visitor can try next. If a search returned nothing, keep their original query visible and offer a way to clear it.

The same principle applies to error messages: say what happened in plain language and offer a useful next step. Avoid blaming the person who clicked.

## Make interaction visible

A hover treatment helps mouse users discover links, but it should never be the only sign that something is clickable. Use recognizable labels, readable contrast, and keyboard-visible focus states.

For a copy button, update the label after success. For a mobile navigation menu, give the control an accessible name and let the Escape key close it. These aren't finishing touches; they're part of the interaction.

## Choose a small visual vocabulary

A restrained set of type sizes, spacing rules, and colors gives content room to breathe. One strong accent is often more useful than a collection of equally loud colors.

The point is consistency, not sameness. Repeating patterns reduces work for readers: once a card, tag, or section heading makes sense, the next one does too.

## Build for the second visit

A beautiful first impression matters. A site that's easy to scan, comfortable to read, and predictable on every screen gives someone a reason to return.
