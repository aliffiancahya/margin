There's a particular energy that comes with opening a new project. The blank canvas feels full of possibility, and it's easy to imagine every feature at once. Finishing asks for a different skill: knowing what belongs in version one.

## Write down the promise

Before choosing packages, finish the sentence: “Someone can use this site to…” For a journal, it might be “find and read articles about web development.” That promise defines the essential experience.

A home page, an organized archive, clear article pages, and navigation deliver it. A complex account system probably doesn't.

## Work in complete slices

Build a page until it works on a narrow screen and a keyboard, then move on. Half-finished features spread across six routes are harder to evaluate than one complete journey from the home page to an article.

Keep a short list of rough edges and address the ones a visitor will encounter first.

## Make the handoff easy

A README that explains setup, configuration, and content editing matters. So do meaningful metadata, a working not-found state, and a project that starts without secret local services.

Small, complete projects show your decisions more clearly than big projects that rely on promises about what will be added later.
